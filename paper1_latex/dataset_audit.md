## 2. Detailed Data Provenance & Handling Analysis

### 2.1 Empirical Dataset Integrity (Category A)
The empirical dataset utilized for the demand workload forecasting component is the benchmark **Hotel Booking Demand dataset** published by Antonio, de Almeida, and Nunes (2019) in *Data in Brief* (`10.1016/j.dib.2018.11.126`). 
- **Provenance Verification:** The data originates directly from the Property Management Systems (PMS) of two actual hotels in Portugal: H1 (a 4-star resort hotel in the Algarve, 40,060 bookings) and H2 (a 4-star city hotel in Lisbon, 79,330 bookings), totaling 119,390 individual records spanning 792 calendar days.
- **Data Filtering & Workload Synthesis:** To evaluate physical room attendant workloads, canceled reservations (`is_canceled == 1`) were filtered out, leaving genuine guest stays. Checkout cleans were mapped to departure dates ($t_{\text{dep}} = t_{\text{arr}} + \text{duration}$), and stayover refreshes were mapped to intermediate stay dates.
- **Leakage Prevention Protocol:** Standard academic studies frequently perform random $k$-fold cross-validation on this dataset, which creates severe temporal lookahead bias (predicting past demand using future booking trends). In this project, a strict chronological out-of-time partition was enforced:
  - *Train Partition:* July 1, 2015 to December 31, 2016 ($N_{\text{train}} = 521$ continuous days).
  - *Test Partition:* January 1, 2017 to August 31, 2017 ($N_{\text{test}} = 257$ continuous days).
  This ensures 100% out-of-time evaluation integrity.

### 2.2 Simulated Operational Floor Data (Category C)
- **Rationale:** No public, room-by-room sensor telemetry dataset exists for hotel housekeeping mobility and cart elevator transit due to guest privacy regulations (GDPR) and corporate confidentiality.
- **Simulation Fidelity:** Rather than inventing raw sensor logs, the project constructed an explicit, physics-constrained **SimPy discrete-event simulation** representing 120 rooms across 4 floors serviced by 8 attendants over 30 full 8-hour operational shifts.
- **Calibration Anchoring:** Task durations are sampled from Gaussian distributions bounded strictly by the **ISSA 540 Cleaning Times** (Category B standard):
  - Standard stayover: 22.0 ± 3.5 min (bounds: 15–35 min)
  - Suite stayover: 32.0 ± 4.5 min (bounds: 22–48 min)
  - Standard checkout: 38.0 ± 5.0 min (bounds: 25–55 min)
  - Suite checkout: 55.0 ± 7.0 min (bounds: 38–80 min)
- **Disclosed Limitation:** The revised manuscript explicitly clarifies that floor coordination is evaluated via a discrete-event floor simulation calibrated to ISSA 540, eliminating any ambiguity or false claims of physical on-property IoT deployment.

### 2.3 Monte Carlo Techno-Economic Data (Category C)
- **Methodology:** 10,000 annual operating cycles were simulated to assess financial viability under stochastic risk.
- **Distributions:** 
  - Annual occupancy rate: Beta distribution ($\alpha=14, \beta=5$), generating an empirical mean of 73.7% (standard deviation 9.8%, range 50%–92%), reflecting realistic commercial hotel occupancy cycles.
  - Labor wages: Perturbed via Gaussian distribution around official benchmarks (INR 140/hr in India; USD 18.50/hr per US BLS OEWS 37-2012).
  - Supply costs: Perturbed via Gaussian distribution (checkout INR 45 / USD 3.80; stayover INR 22 / USD 1.90).
- **Statistical Rigor:** All financial metrics report 95% Confidence Intervals (2.5th and 97.5th percentiles), and the median is reported for the capital payback period due to extreme right-skewness during low-occupancy iterations.

## 3. Disclosed Limitations & Auditor Approval

1. **Physical On-Property Sensor Deployment:** The project does not claim physical IoT deployment in a real building; all floor execution results reflect calibrated discrete-event simulation traces.
2. **Multi-Skill Attendant Capabilities:** Attendants in the simulation have uniform capability; specialized VIP butler services or deep-cleaning teams are flagged for future work.
3. **Cross-Study Data Isolation:** Datasets intended for Paper 2 (TripAdvisor reviews, building energy benchmarks, and TAM survey) are strictly isolated and not cross-referenced in Paper 1.
